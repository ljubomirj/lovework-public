# The LoveWork Manifesto

Most people are capable of much more than their working lives allow them to realise. This is not usually a failure of talent. It is often a failure of attention, timing, information, confidence, circumstance, and access to the right people.

We search for work episodically: a few hurried weeks every few years, often while money and patience are running out. Looking for work, both paid-a job, and unpaid, is a full time job of its own. Organisations search continuously.  Recruiters search continuously. Markets move continuously. The person seeking meaningful work is the only participant expected to treat discovery as an occasional side project.

People with exceptional representation live differently. Actors, writers, athletes, and public figures have agents who understand them, notice openings, make introductions, protect their interests, and think beyond the next paid engagement. Artificial intelligence makes it possible to extend some of that care and continuity beyond the famous and wealthy.

LoveWork is an attempt to build that personal agent.

## The principal—agent relationship

We call the person LoveWork serves a **principal**, not a *candidate*. The word “candidate” comes from the company’s point of view: recruiters, working for the company, use it for people they may pay to work. It centres the hiring side.

LoveWork takes the other point of view. You are the principal. The LoveWork agent advises and works for you: it investigates opportunities, protects your time and attention, keeps your evidence private, and helps you decide what to do. It does not represent the employer or treat you as inventory for the labour market.

The relationship is symbiotic, but not symmetrical. It is a principal—agent relationship: the principal authorises the work, owns the information, makes the decisions, and judges whether the agent has been useful. The agent can learn from feedback and outcomes, but it does not decide on the principal’s behalf.

It should know more than the words on a current CV. It should understand the long path that produced the person, the constraints of their present life, the work they refuse to do, and several futures they might plausibly grow into. It should look for jobs, but also projects, collaborators, challenges, grants, residencies, companies, and possibilities that do not fit a job board.

It should not optimise for application volume. It should protect the person's time and attention. It should remember what has already been tried, learn from reactions and outcomes, and become a better judge over years rather than reset at the start of every search.

The important question is not simply, "Can this person get this job?" It is:

> Would a future version of this person be glad that they pursued this
> opportunity now?

## Why not another recruitment and or screeneing tool

People applying for jobs is N people trying to get M musical chairs with N>>M. That is a waste of human talent, opportunity loss, gains not pursued on the part of the society. In addition to a small personal loss that is every time a candidate spends time pours their hopes in an application only to be disappronted, or even worse - ignored, as insiginficant as a character put on "Mute", in their personal story. It's like dating where one side treats it an 1:1 realtion, while for the other treats it an N:1. Every rejection saps the confidence, every application unanswered saps the will for a human to continue the game the chase. As long it's a chase on one side only - it's a losing proposition for that side. Labour has been losing value for some time now. Disasters like plagues, or world wars, may shift power from the few to the many when the balance of supply and demand is disbalanced - but that doesn't last. A determined well organised group with a clear aim even when smaller in numbers, is more powerful than an amorphous aimless unorganised collection of indeviduals, even when latter greater in numbers. Connections not individual nodes is there power of coordination lies. Increasing the spamming rate may achieve relative advantage, but at a macro level - we all worse off in the 'greaer happiness to greater numbers of people' stakes. Sovereign indivdual, not an obediant worker, is what next century calls for.

## Serendipity, luck on the wings

A personal agent that only scrapes job websites is a better search box.  Useful, but not enough. Most pivotal work — the role that redirects a career, the collaborator that changes a company, the introduction made a year early — never reaches a job board. It moves through people.

A network of N people carries on the order of N² possible connections, and every one of them is a door someone could open for someone else.  That quadratic surface is the principal's greatest tool against entropy: relationships cool, favours go unreturned, and what people told us they were looking for a year ago quietly expires. An agent that tends this surface — remembers who helped and when the help was never reciprocated, keeps alive what people said they wanted, and suggests the introduction both sides would welcome — is doing something no job website can.

Each wave of technology has made connection cheaper. Nokia's "Connecting people" was the first swallow of an early spring. The Internet made reach possible; social media made it ambient; AI now makes the missing layer possible — curation and introduction at the scale of one's own life. For the first time, an agent can sit on top of everything its principal has met, mailed, and worked with, and increase the luck surface area for them and for the people around them: surfacing the opportunities that already exist across the network, before they expire unconnected.

This does not replace the aim stated below. The point is not to swap luck for intention, but to aim intention at the conditions under which good things tend to happen — to make lucky coincidence more likely, on purpose.

LoveWork will begin imperfectly. Its judgments will be contestable, its model of a person incomplete, and its machinery unfinished. That is a reason to make the work visible early: so the premise can be examined, the design improved, and the agent shaped around real human lives rather than an abstract labour market.

The aim is simple to state and difficult to achieve: help more people realise their potential intentionally, rather than only by lucky coincidence.

---

> **Work on what you like — love your work.**

*The motto's arc:* the liking comes first, and can only come first — before the work exists there is nothing to love, only a field you find interesting and choose to labour in. The love arrives later, directed at what the working deposits: your work, the oeuvre, the thing you made. And sometimes you gift that to the world — and like that too.

*Every worker a principal.*
