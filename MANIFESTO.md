# The LoveWork Manifesto

Most people are capable of much more than their working lives allow them to
realise. This is not usually a failure of talent. It is often a failure of
attention, timing, information, confidence, circumstance, and access to the
right people.

We search for work episodically: a few hurried weeks every few years, often
while money and patience are running out. Organisations search continuously.
Recruiters search continuously. Markets move continuously. The person seeking
meaningful work is the only participant expected to treat discovery as an
occasional side project.

People with exceptional representation live differently. Actors, writers,
athletes, and public figures have agents who understand them, notice openings,
make introductions, protect their interests, and think beyond the next paid
engagement. Artificial intelligence makes it possible to extend some of that
care and continuity beyond the famous and wealthy.

LoveWork is an attempt to build that personal agent.

It should know more than the words on a current CV. It should understand the
long path that produced the person, the constraints of their present life, the
work they refuse to do, and several futures they might plausibly grow into. It
should look for jobs, but also projects, collaborators, challenges, grants,
residencies, companies, and possibilities that do not fit a job board.

It should not optimise for application volume. It should protect the person's
time and attention. It should remember what has already been tried, learn from
reactions and outcomes, and become a better judge over years rather than reset
at the start of every search.

The important question is not simply, "Can this person get this job?" It is:

> Would a future version of this person be glad that they pursued this
> opportunity now?

LoveWork will begin imperfectly. Its judgments will be contestable, its model
of a person incomplete, and its machinery unfinished. That is a reason to make
the work visible early: so the premise can be examined, the design improved,
and the agent shaped around real human lives rather than an abstract labour
market.

The aim is simple to state and difficult to achieve: help more people realise
their potential intentionally, rather than by lucky coincidence.
