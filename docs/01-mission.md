# Chapter 01 — Mission

> **Audience:** everyone. Read this first.
> **See also:** [`../README.md`](../README.md) for the short pitch.

## Why this exists

**LoveWork** is the mission. **LoveWork** is the product.

> *Work that you love, so you never work a day in your life.*

All humans are capable of this. Most don't discover enough — or they run out
of time. LoveWork is on a mission to speed that up 1000×. Humans are built to
do work they love; they are *destroyed* by the lack of meaningful work. The
personal work agent is a small step toward closing the gap between what people
*could* do and what they *end up* doing.

## The 5-point motivation

1. Most people don't reach their potential — they are much more capable than what they achieve. Huge unrealised opportunity.
2. A personal work agent will seek opportunities for the person 24/7; very different to looking for a job oneself a few months every few years, while trying to outrun the depletion of savings to zero, homelessness, hunger, and ultimately — death.
3. A personal work agent vs personal job search is like the difference between a personal workout coach and making yourself go to the gym.
4. Yes, a personal agent is at present the stuff of movie stars and best-selling-book writers; now the AI implementation will make it available to all.
5. So people realise their full potential *intentionally* — not by lucky coincidence.

## The critique it answers

> The "follow your dreams" / "anyone can make it" ethos is good for the system
> but bad for the individual (LindyMan, @PaulSkallas). It produces some
> interesting winners for society — startups, products, services — but it
> causes a lot of misery for individuals, since most people end up working jobs
> they would rather not.

LoveWork pushes back on that asymmetry: the system profits when people stay
under-matched; the person doesn't. The agent works for the *individual*, not
the labour market. Findings are private — a finding the principal sees is a
finding only they see, and the advantage over the general applicant pool is
that they are not competing with 1000 other applicants for the same lead.

## Principal and agent

LoveWork deliberately uses **principal**, not *candidate*, for the person it
serves. “Candidate” is the employer or recruiter’s word for a worker being
considered; it centres the hiring side. In LoveWork, the person authorises the
work, owns the information and decides what action to take. They are the
principal. LoveWork is their agent: it investigates, recommends, records
evidence and carries out only the work within that authorisation.

This is a symbiotic but asymmetric principal—agent relationship. A good agent
improves the principal’s choices and learns from their feedback and outcomes;
it does not become an employer-side recruiter, decide on the principal’s
behalf, or treat their profile and history as market inventory. We preserve
*candidate* only when quoting or interoperating with an external
employer-owned system.

## What LoveWork is *not*

- **Not a narrow job-search engine.** A job is work we do for someone else, for
  money. LoveWork is after work that maybe a job too, but might not be —
  projects, collaborations, founding roles, contracts. The branches in a
  principal's `possibilities.md` (see [chapter 03](03-profiles.md)) make this
  explicit per person.
- **Not a recruiter / aggregator for the labour market.** It works for one
  principal at a time. Multi-principal service is a Phase-3 shape
  ([chapter 08](08-decisions-history.md)), but even then each principal's
  findings stay their own.
- **Not a job-board scraper.** It crawls org career pages directly, scores
  against a profile, and tracks lifecycle over time. The wiki it writes is a
  *personal* memory of every role ever seen at every org — months of tracking,
  not a one-shot listing.

## The two operating modes

LoveWork serves two kinds of principal, and the difference matters for how to
read the output:

- **Targeted-match principals (lj, kj):** they know their profession. The agent
  optimises for the best fit against a known profession and known role types.
  A GO means "apply today".

- **Emergent-profession principals (vj, pk):** the profession is *not yet
  chosen*. The agent casts a wide net across many seed branches and grows a
  thicket of plausible listings; clusters of fit in the thicket are *principal
  professions*. The matcher leans toward MAYBE over KILL because a wider net
  produces better clusters. A GO here means "this is data about a possible
  profession", not necessarily "apply today". See [chapter 03](03-profiles.md).
