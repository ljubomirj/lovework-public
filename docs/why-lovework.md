# Why LoveWork?

## Job search is episodic; opportunity is continuous

Most people look seriously for work only when they need a new job. That search
begins under pressure and ends when an acceptable offer appears. It rarely has
the time horizon needed to notice unusual collaborations, emerging fields, or
roles that require months of preparation.

Meanwhile, organisations, recruiters, and professional networks search
continuously. Opportunity has an always-on supply side and an intermittent,
overloaded human demand side.

LoveWork makes discovery continuous for the person too.

## A CV describes evidence, not possibility

A CV is a useful compressed account of work already done. It is a poor model
of what someone could do next. It omits abandoned interests, unusual
combinations of skills, non-linear life experience, constraints, motivations,
and plausible futures that have not yet produced a job title.

Matching a vacancy to keywords in a CV therefore answers only a narrow
question. LoveWork instead asks how an opportunity relates to the whole person
and the directions in which they may grow.

## Search should include more than vacancies

Meaningful work may arrive as:

- a conventional role;
- a research problem or challenge;
- a collaborator looking for a particular complement;
- a commission, residency, grant, or fellowship;
- a young organisation not yet hiring conventionally;
- a project worth initiating rather than joining.

The source set should expand as the agent understands the person better.

## Judgment must improve over time

A recommendation system that forgets every rejection, hesitation, changed
priority, and successful choice cannot represent someone well. LoveWork treats
the sequence of recommendations, decisions, and outcomes as a longitudinal
learning problem.

The aim is not maximum activity. It is better judgment: fewer irrelevant
interruptions, more timely possibilities, and work that the person is more
likely to value in retrospect.
